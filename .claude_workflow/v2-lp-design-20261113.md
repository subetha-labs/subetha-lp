Exception marker (HTML, Markdown, plain text alike): `<!-- claims-ok rule=<rule> text="<exact assertion text>"
reason=denial|limitation|plan|technical -->`. Validation:

- the check runs on the public text: comments removed, tags replaced by spaces, entities decoded; markers are read
  before removal and keep their position, so the assertion-level scope survives comment removal;
- in HTML a source newline is a space (as rendered); comments, `<wbr>` and inline tags (`code`, `strong`, `em`,
  `span`, `a`, …) are removed without inserting a space so that "anon<!-- -->ymous", "anon<wbr>ymous",
  "anon<span>ymous</span>" and "fully <code>private</code>" read as rendered, and only block-level tag boundaries (`p`, `h1`–`h6`, `li`, `summary`, `td`, `div`, `br`, …) become
  line breaks; a literal space in a trigger matches any whitespace, so a wrapped line cannot split a trigger; an
  *assertion* is the text between two of `. ! ? 。 ！ ？` or line breaks, and its *context* is the text between two
  of `. ! ? 。 ！ ？` only (line breaks ignored); every trigger occurrence in every assertion must be consumed by
  exactly one exception, and unconsumed occurrences fail;
- an assertion whose context is wider than itself (a formatting boundary split a sentence that carries other
  words, such as "It is false that<br>SubEtha is not anonymous.") is rejected as ambiguous for any exception; the
  eligibility gates below run on the context, so a reversing or affirming frame on the other side of a `<br>`, a
  newline or a tag boundary is still seen;
- a marker's `text` must equal the complete sentence that holds the trigger (whitespace-normalised), not a
  substring of it, so the eligibility gates below always see the whole assertion; a marker binds to the first
  unconsumed trigger occurrence of its rule that starts after the marker (within 600 characters) and whose sentence
  equals `text`; a `text` containing two trigger occurrences is rejected; so a denial marker can never consume an
  affirmation in a neighbouring sentence, in another clause with its own trigger, or on the same line;
- eligibility, evaluated on the context for markers and external entries alike: the context must not be
  an affirmative predication of a prohibited property (for example "is anonymous", "are hidden", 「は匿名です」 are
  rejected under every reason) and must not contain a meaning-reversing frame (`it is false/untrue/not true/wrong
  that`, `no longer`, `not just/only/merely`, `except`, 「誤り」「間違い」「とは限らない」「だけでなく」「もはや」),
  which is rejected as ambiguous; `denial` requires a negation that applies to the property itself: a negating word
  at most three words before the trigger, a negated verb at most six words after it, or (JA) a negative ending in
  the same clause, in one of these shapes only (double negations and exception frames such as "not without",
  "anything but", 「わけでは」 are rejected as ambiguous): the negating word directly before the property ("not anonymous",
  "no real anonymity"), a coordination the negation covers ("neither anonymity nor unlinkability"), a negated
  property verb whose object it is ("does not guarantee unlinkability or anonymity"), the property as subject head (at most one short prepositional
  phrase after it) of a negated property verb or of "is not claimed / guaranteed / provided …" ("amount privacy
  does not carry over"; not "anonymous payments do not imply delays", where the property only modifies the subject),
  or in Japanese the negated property verb right after the property with at most one particle and no further
  object (「匿名性を保証しません」「金額は隠れません」「金額の秘匿性は引き継がれません」, also when coordinated:
  「…られないことや匿名性を保証しません」). "Anonymous payments are not delayed", "No registration for anonymous
  payments", 「匿名決済に登録が要りません」 and 「匿名決済は遅延を保証しません」 are rejected; under every reason a verb that bestows the property (provides, guarantees, delivers, 提供, 保証 …) and
  is not itself negated rejects the exception, except in a question answered with No (`no`, `not`, `never`, `cannot`, `without`, 「ない」「ません」「いいえ」「ず」…) or a question whose next
  sentence starts with "No" / 「いいえ」; `limitation` requires the same property-bound negation, or the property as subject
  of a negative predicate ("… is not quantified", "… is unverified", "… is out of scope", 「…は未検証」; "is
  verified", a nearby "within limits" or "with unverified latency" do not qualify); `plan` requires the property itself to be what is planned ("plan to add / offer /
  support / extend to …", "… is planned", 「…を計画」), not a plan that mentions or improves it; `technical` requires the term to be quoted in the source
  (quotes, backticks or `<code>`) and discussed as a term ("the word …", "is used", 「という語」); it is not used on
  this page;
- a sentence with several clauses is handled per trigger: each trigger occurrence needs its own eligible
  exception, so "it is not anonymous, but it is untraceable" fails on the second clause;
- trigger occurrences inside attribute values (`alt`, `title`, `content`, `aria-label`, `placeholder`) cannot be
  exempted;
- for public files this task may not edit, the script carries an external table of per-occurrence exceptions
  (file, rule, exact sentence fragment, reason, expected count) under the same eligibility gates; the entries for a
  file must match exactly the number of occurrences they declare (fewer or more actual occurrences both fail), each
  entry consumes exactly one occurrence, and no entry exempts another assertion on the same line or in the same
  sentence. No committed exception record contains a private repository URL, a hostname, a path or a credential.

Fixtures (`tests/claims/`, EN and JA): `pass-*` (a denial with a marker, a limitation with a marker, a labelled
plan without triggers, "anonymity set", an official `@x402/*` install) and `fail-*` (a bare affirmation for every
rule in both languages; the allowed denial and a forbidden affirmation on one line with only the denial marked, in
EN, in JA and in a mixed EN/JA line; a denial and an affirmation as two clauses of one sentence, EN and JA; a
falsely labelled affirmation (`reason=denial` on "is anonymous" / 「は匿名です」), EN and JA; a negation that does not
apply to the property ("Enjoy anonymous payments without delays" / 「匿名決済を提供し、待ち時間はありません」); an
unquoted `technical` affirmation; a `limitation` with only a generic cue; a reversed frame
("it is false that … is not anonymous" / 「…ではないというのは誤り」) with a denial marker, EN and JA; a marker whose
`text` is a substring rather than the whole sentence; a reversing frame separated from the assertion by `<br>`
(HTML) and by a newline (plain text), EN and JA; a marker whose `text` spans two sentences with one trigger; a marker whose `text` contains two triggers; a marker with the wrong rule; a
marker farther than 600 characters; a trigger in an `alt` attribute with a marker). `check-claims.py --fixtures`
asserts each `pass-*` passes and each `fail-*` fails with the expected message. Fixtures hold synthetic text only,
and every `fail-*` fixture is stored base64-encoded (`*.html.b64`, `*.txt.b64`) and decoded in memory, so the public
repository holds no plaintext prohibited claim or D3 shape anywhere.

## 5. Output checks (`scripts/check-private-source-links.py`)

- `PRIVATE_REPOS` gains the v2 product repository entry named in the brief plus a synthetic fixture entry; the
  check scans `href` values and raw text of every inventory file for links to the private product repositories
  (D4). The eight pre-existing links in the archive page are carried as a count-fixed external exception that
  references the `PRIVATE_REPOS` entry by identifier, never by URL (file, repository identifier, expected count 8,
  reason: V1 archive, parent decision 2026-11-13, tracked separately); seven or nine occurrences fail. The same
  form covers the one pre-existing occurrence each in `scripts/check-mobile-scope.py` and the header-cleanup note
  for the repository-wide pass (§6).
- The V1 `HISTORY` constant and the V1 FAQ/demo assertions are replaced by the v2 URL contract (§2), the required
  ids, headline A, the subline qualifiers, the hosted-configuration captions, and the absence of `#history`,
  Poseidon, permit, payment history, participant-scoped, proof-gated and the V1 lifecycle words.
- Every file in the inventory and in the repository is scanned, whatever its name or type: its bytes are decoded
  as UTF-8 with undecodable bytes replaced, and the exact-match categories (digest denylist, hosts under the
  company domain, faucet URLs, 40-hex addresses, credential values, asset amounts, private links, claims
  triggers) run on that text. Only the tokenisation heuristics that are meaningless on byte noise (path templates,
  one-letter segments, route segments, currency symbols) are skipped for content that is not UTF-8. Classification
  (text, or a structurally validated PNG/ICO/JPEG) is reported in the inventory and still fails closed on an
  unclassifiable file; it never exempts a file from the scans. D3 scans run on everything retrievable as text,
  including HTML comments, script and style bodies and attribute values, not only on the rendered text.
- D4 and faucet matching run on the fully decoded source (entities, then percent escapes, protocol-relative
  `//host` spelled out as https, dot segments resolved, explicit ports and userinfo parsed), which spells out
  encoded links without adding or removing occurrences, so counts stay exact and encoded links cannot slip past.
- D3 categories, each with a negative fixture in `tests/output/` using synthetic values. Because this repository
  is public, the known hosted hostnames, resource/recovery/facilitator paths, the tenant path template, credential
  variable and header names and the runbook script names are **not written literally in the checker**: it
  normalises every hostname label, URL path segment, shell-looking token and `NAME=` variable in the output and
  compares SHA-256 digests against a digest denylist (the plaintext list stays with the parent). Shape rules are
  literal: any host under the company domain outside the public allowlist (LP, Docs, Router and the two Router
  demo providers already public on `/router/`); facilitator-style route segments (`verify`, `settle`, `supported`,
  `orders`) inside any URL path; any URL path containing a placeholder (`<…>`, `:name`, `{name}`) or a one-letter
  segment followed by an identifier; URLs whose host or path contains `faucet`; 40-hex addresses; authorization
  header values; prices and probe amounts (a number followed by an asset or atomic-unit word, or a sub-cent asset amount; pre-existing Router product prices on `/router/` are carried as count-fixed per-file exceptions). A claims marker never exempts a D3 hit.
- The third-party test-network faucet links in `router/llms.txt` (three distinct public URLs, six occurrences;
  `router/index.html` has only the word, no URL) are outside D3 (spec rev 4, D3 row) and pass through
  per-occurrence external exceptions (file, public URL, reason, expected count 4 / 1 / 1); any other faucet URL,
  or a changed count, fails.

## 8. Threat model and review scope (parent decision, 2026-11-13)

The guards protect against inadvertent drift by cooperating authors (the owner's future sessions and reviewers),
not against an adversarial author. Review scope: (i) public content, that is claims against the spec §2/§3, EN/JA
parity, D3/D4 leaks into public output, hosted/self-run blending, the URL contract and the CI markers; (ii) the
guards, that is coverage of the categories, fixtures, publication boundary and count-fixed exceptions the spec §3/§5
requires. Deliberate bypass constructions (encoding tricks, markup splitting, malformed binaries, double negations,
DNS spelling variants and the like) are out of scope unless they fall under a required category; when found they
are listed as residual hardening items and do not affect the verdict.
